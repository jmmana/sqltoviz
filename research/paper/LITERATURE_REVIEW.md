# Related work (E08-01, #89) - full-text review, version 2

## Method, provenance and limits
- 23 references were verified by `tools/verify_references.py` against arXiv/Crossref (exact title + first-author match; `related_work/verification_report.json`); BibTeX is generated only from those responses.
- **Full texts of 11 of the 12 core works were read** (notes with exact numbers and the place where each appears: `related_work/notes/*.md`). The reading was agent-assisted (three independent readers with a "report only what the paper says, cite the location, mark what is not accessible" protocol); I spot-checked six numbers against the PDFs (VizML CARS 88.96 and dedup counts 1,066,443 / 2,884,437 / 119,815 / 287,416; Table2Charts 266,252 charts and 43.84 % R@1; Draco 2 86 % agreement and 18/40 of 147 constraints), all matching. Section numbers in some notes were inferred from heading order.
- **Show Me (Mackinlay et al., 2007) could not be read**: the available links returned HTML, not the paper; it is cited at title level only. Four 2022-2024 works (Prompt4Vis, VisEval, TaskVis, Nguyen et al.) were found by a logged arXiv search (`related_work/search_log.json`) and are described from their abstracts only.
- The search was not exhaustive (six arXiv queries, 8 results each; no ACM/IEEE database search). **No novelty claim is made.**

## Findings that matter for our design and claims
1. **Metrics are not comparable across works**: balanced accuracy (VizML), recall of user-made charts (Table2Charts), tree-match accuracy on NL input (nvBench/ncNet), NDCG/F-measure on a student-labelled pool (DeepEye), pair accuracy (Draco), user-study counts (Voyager). Any comparison with our numbers would be apples to oranges; we therefore report majority-class baselines and per-class supports, and make no cross-paper numeric comparison.
2. **Label semantics differ**: VizML/Table2Charts learn what users *created*; nvBench gold labels are *synthesized candidates* (valid per rules of thumb and filtered by DeepEye), not a unique best chart; ours are synthetic (convention), LLM, and (pending) human preferences. Class imbalance is severe in public data (nvBench: bar 76 %; Table2Charts: line 43 %, bar 25 %), so majority baselines are strong.
3. **No reviewed work takes the SQL text together with the executed result set as input, and none pairs a learned ranker with hard semantic eligibility constraints that fail closed**; VizML/Table2Charts/DeepEye use only the table, nvBench/ncNet/LIDA start from natural language. This is an observation about 11 read works plus 4 abstracts, not a proof of novelty.
4. **Reproducibility of baselines is limited**: DeepEye and VizML papers state no release; Table2Charts code was promised after review; our attempt to obtain VizML data failed (HTTP 403, no license; DATASET_LICENSE_AUDIT.md). We therefore cannot rerun those systems on our data.
5. **Benchmark validity is itself questioned** in the literature: Nguyen et al. (abstract) report that text-to-vis benchmarks do not test the same distribution of chart types, attributes and actions as real-world code, with one dataset representative but needing extensive modification. This supports treating public-benchmark scores (including ours on Spider-derived queries, which are ~70 % table-type results) with caution.
6. Four recent works address LLM/NL visualization generation (Prompt4Vis, VisEval: 2,524 queries over 146 databases per its abstract) and task-oriented rule-based recommendation (TaskVis: 18 analytic tasks, answer-set programming). Their full texts were not read.
